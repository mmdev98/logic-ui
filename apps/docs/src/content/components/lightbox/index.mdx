## Usage guidelines

- **The images fly:** the image grows out of its trigger when the viewer opens and flies back when it closes, to its own trigger or to the one `closeTarget` picks, such as a "+5" tile. Hide the trigger while it has `data-popup-open`.
- **One gesture engine:** swipe, pinch, double tap, pan and pull to dismiss are handled together, so they never fight over a touch. A long drag can cross multiple slides and settles at the position reached, rather than advancing only one. The viewer needs no carousel or zoom library.
- **Your layout, your items:** the lightbox renders no thumbnails of its own. Lay out your page however you like and place a `Lightbox.Trigger` per item. Items can have any shape; the lightbox only needs a stable value for each.
- **No dependencies:** the lightbox needs no carousel, zoom, animation or image library, and no framework. `Lightbox.Media` wraps your own `<img>`, `<video>` or `<iframe>`; give it any `src` or `srcSet`.

## Examples

### Getting started

The smallest viewer: triggers, the images and a close button. Swipe, pinch, double tap, pull to dismiss and the keyboard all work.

<Demo src="./demos/minimal.tsx" />

`Lightbox.Root` takes the items in `items`, with any shape. Each item needs a value that identifies it and stays the same when items are added, removed or reordered: by default its `id`, or whatever `itemToValue` returns. `Lightbox.Trigger` opens the viewer on the item with its `value`. `Lightbox.Viewport` calls its function child for the item shown and its neighbours; the parts take what they need as props. Your `<img>` gets its `src` and `alt`, and `Lightbox.Media` gets the natural `width` and `height`, which give it the right shape before the image loads.

```tsx title="Items"
const photos = [
  { id: "1", src: "/photos/1.jpg", width: 1600, height: 1067, alt: "Valley" },
  { id: "2", src: "/photos/2.jpg", alt: "Portrait" },
];

<Lightbox.Root items={photos}>
  {photos.map((photo) => (
    <Lightbox.Trigger key={photo.id} value={photo.id}>
      <img src={photo.thumb} alt="" />
    </Lightbox.Trigger>
  ))}
  <Lightbox.Portal>
    <Lightbox.Backdrop />
    <Lightbox.Popup>
      <Lightbox.Viewport>
        {(photo) => (
          <Lightbox.Slide>
            <Lightbox.Media width={photo.width} height={photo.height}>
              <img src={photo.src} alt={photo.alt} />
            </Lightbox.Media>
          </Lightbox.Slide>
        )}
      </Lightbox.Viewport>
      <Lightbox.Close />
    </Lightbox.Popup>
  </Lightbox.Portal>
</Lightbox.Root>;

// Items without an `id`:
<Lightbox.Root items={files} itemToValue={(file) => file.path}>
  …
</Lightbox.Root>;
```

### Where the image lands

When the viewer closes, the image flies back to a trigger on the page, and focus returns to it. `closeTarget` on `Lightbox.Root` picks which:

- `"active"` (the default): the trigger of the item shown. Without one on the page, the image fades out where it is.
- `"opener"`: the trigger that opened the viewer, whichever item is shown. For a cover that opens a whole album, like the ones below.
- `"nearest"`: the trigger of the item shown, or else the trigger of the nearest item that has one. For a grid that shows a few items and a "+5" tile, like the gallery at the top of the page.

A trigger that shows something other than its media takes `crossfade`: an avatar for a story, or a tile with a "+5" count or a play badge, which then fade out as the image leaves and back in as it lands. With it, the image grows out of its look as the viewer opens, and turns back into it on the way home, like the stories below.

The trigger the image will fly back to has `data-popup-open` the whole time the viewer is open, following the item shown: hide it (`data-popup-open:invisible`) and its slot waits empty, for the drop after a drag too. When that trigger shows another image (the cover, the "+5" tile), a copy of it flies along with the image and fades in over it, so the image turns into the trigger on the way, and lands looking exactly like it. A trigger that can't be seen, scrolled out of the window or out of a list that clips it, gets the image faded out instead; `scrollToTrigger` scrolls the page to it beforehand, as in the chat above.

### Instagram-style stories

The people stay in one horizontally scrollable row on every screen size. Their rings change color and opacity on hover without lifting the avatars. The demo uses `select-none` to avoid accidental selection, except for the reply field, which keeps `select-text`.

Each ring is a person, and the items of the lightbox are the people: swipe, or drag past the edge of the card, to go from one person to the next. A person's own stories play inside their slide, like Instagram:

- **They play by themselves.** One bar per story fills for five seconds, or for the length of a video, then the next story shows, then the next person. After the last one, the viewer closes, and the ring turns grey.
- **Tap** the left third of the card to go back, anywhere else to go on; ← → do the same. **Hold** to pause; the controls fade while you hold. The pause button and typing a reply pause too, as WCAG asks of content that moves on by itself.
- **Controlled.** The stories move on to the next person by changing `value`, and the viewer slides there as it does for Next; after the last person, `open` turns `false`. `onOpenChangeComplete` starts the bars once the card has landed. The viewer loads the story the person is on before the card flies out of their ring.
- **Keys of your own.** The popup's `onKeyDown` runs before its shortcuts: it takes ← → for the stories and prevents the default, so the library doesn't also change the person.

The rings take `crossfade`, since they show a face rather than the story: the card grows out of the face as it opens, and turns back into it as it flies home.

Every control sits on the story card, inside `Lightbox.Media`: the bars, the author, pause, `Lightbox.Close`, the story's text in a `Lightbox.Caption`, and the reply field. So they slide with the person during a swipe, follow the pull to dismiss, and fly back into the ring with the card. Controls inside a slide get their clicks: a press that starts on a button, a link or a field isn't a gesture. `maxScale={1}` keeps the stories at their resting scale.

The stories turn like the faces of a cube, in CSS only. Each `Lightbox.Slide` has `--lightbox-slide-progress`, its distance from the centre in slides (`0` shown, `-1` one to the left, `1` one to the right), on every frame of a swipe, its settle and Next. The library places slides with `translate`, so their `transform` is free: here each person turns by the progress times 90° around the edge they share with the next one, with a `perspective` on `Lightbox.Viewport`. The same variable makes a fade (`opacity`), a stack, or a coverflow.

```tsx title="Cube transition"
<Lightbox.Viewport className="[perspective:1400px] gap-x-0">
  {(story) => (
    <Lightbox.Slide className="[transform-origin:clamp(0%,calc(50%_-_var(--lightbox-slide-progress)*10000%),100%)_50%] [transform:rotateY(calc(var(--lightbox-slide-progress)*90deg))] [backface-visibility:hidden]">
      …
    </Lightbox.Slide>
  )}
</Lightbox.Viewport>
```

### 3D coverflow

The same variable makes a coverflow. `neighbors={3}` on `Lightbox.Viewport` renders three covers on each side, instead of one. The covers sit closer together than the viewport is wide, so `--lightbox-swipe-distance` on the viewport sets how far the finger travels for one cover, as any CSS length (`[--lightbox-swipe-distance:calc(var(--cover-size)*2.2)]`), and the covers keep up with the finger instead of lagging behind it. Slides placed side by side don't need it, even when the media is smaller than the viewport: the media moves with its slide. While you hold a cover, the viewport has `data-swiping`; raising it (`data-swiping:z-10`) draws the cover above its reflection and the controls.

The structure separates four jobs:

- `Lightbox.Slide` places the cover in the fan and sorts it by depth. Placement stays outside perspective, so depth does not stretch the spacing.
- An inner pose turns the cover in its own `perspective()`, flattened into the slide. A smoothstep curve eases the turn into each wing. The slides stay parallel, so covers overlap without cutting through each other, including halfway through a swipe.
- A surface inside the pose handles the pull and the exit. While the cover shown is pulled to dismiss, the others follow it a little (`--lightbox-dismiss-movement-x` and `-y`), shrink slightly and dim with `--lightbox-dismiss-progress`. On `data-ending-style`, while the active `Lightbox.Media` flies back to its trigger, they fly there too: each cancels its own distance from the centre, moves by `--lightbox-flight-movement-x` and `-y`, and scales down to almost nothing from `--lightbox-flight-scale`, fading out in 60% of `--lightbox-flight-duration`. The ambient background, captions and controls leave on the same timing. Do not transform or fade the active surface or the whole popup: that would move or fade its return flight too.
- A decorative, masked image below the media supplies the reflection without a browser-specific property. It fades on close and zoom. It is outside `Lightbox.Media`, so it never becomes part of the flying image.

The cover size, perspective and depth scale together on smaller screens. The viewport fills the viewer; slide padding sizes and positions the resting covers. This keeps zoom and pan constrained by the viewer edges rather than the small cover box, avoiding empty strips when the enlarged image can fill that axis. While zoomed, neighboring covers and reflections fade away; keep the media's overflow visible so the enlarged image is not clipped. Put the ring and shadow on the image, not `Lightbox.Media`: zoom and pan transform only the image, so a frame on the media box would stay behind. The image inherits the media's rounded corners, including during zoom and the opening and closing flights. Reduced motion uses a flat carousel without reflections or entrance/exit movement.

<Demo src="./demos/coverflow.tsx" />

```tsx title="Coverflow"
// The classes and the progress curve are in the demo's source.
<Lightbox.Viewport
  neighbors={3}
  className="[--lightbox-swipe-distance:calc(var(--cover-size)*2.2)]"
>
  {(album) => (
    <Lightbox.Slide className={local.Slide}>
      <div className={local.Turn}>
        <div className={local.Surface}>
          <Lightbox.Media width={1000} height={1000} className={local.Media}>
            <img
              src={album.src}
              alt={`${album.title} cover`}
              className={local.Image}
            />
          </Lightbox.Media>
          <div aria-hidden="true" className={local.Reflection}>
            <img
              src={album.src}
              alt=""
              draggable={false}
              className={local.ReflectionImage}
            />
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
    </Lightbox.Slide>
  )}
</Lightbox.Viewport>
```

### 3D models

An armory of live 3D weapons, rendered with Google's [`<model-viewer>`](https://modelviewer.dev) and grouped by class. A weapon turns on its tile under the pointer, and they all open in one viewer: drag to rotate the weapon shown, and move between them with the arrows, the cards or ← →. The cards are grouped by class too, and the indicator, corner brackets with a lit bar, glides along with the slides. Escape or the close button flies the weapon back to its tile, turned the way you left it.

`Lightbox.Media` takes any content, so the model's element goes inside it with a `width` and `height` for its shape. Only images zoom, so the zoom gestures leave it alone. Its drags would normally swipe or pull the viewer closed: mark the element with Base UI's `data-base-ui-swipe-ignore` and the viewer leaves every press on it to the model. Set it to `"x"` or `"y"` to keep only the drags along that axis, so a drag along the other one still swipes or pulls, as in a Base UI drawer.

The viewer carries on from the tile, mid-turn and mid-animation, because it shows the same `<model-viewer>` element: the demo creates one per model outside React and moves it between its tile and its slide. Nothing is copied or loaded twice.

- **Ready before it flies.** Before it opens, the viewer is mounted out of sight. A ref callback on each slide moves its model in, and `onOpenChange` returns a Promise that waits until the model shown has drawn itself at the viewer's size. Only then does it fly, so its first frame is already sharp and the right size.
- **Never an empty tile.** A weapon that leaves its tile leaves a still of itself, from `toDataURL()`, in its place. When the viewer closes, the still is taken again, so the tile shows the model as it was turned. Once the model is back and has drawn, the still goes. The thumbnails are stills too.
- **Smooth flights.** Every model holds still during the flights, so it isn't redrawn on every frame of them. Once the viewer has landed, only the model shown moves.
- **The angle you left it at.** On its own, a moved `<model-viewer>` goes back to its `camera-orbit`. The demo writes the current orbit into it before each move.

<Demo src="./demos/models.tsx" />

```tsx title="A 3D model"
<Lightbox.Root
  items={models}
  // The viewer waits for this before the model flies out of its tile.
  onOpenChange={async (open) => {
    if (open) await modelDrawnInViewer();
  }}
>
  …
  <Lightbox.Media width={1} height={1}>
    {/* The model's <model-viewer> moves in here when the slide mounts. */}
    <div ref={hostRef(model.id)} data-base-ui-swipe-ignore="" />
  </Lightbox.Media>
</Lightbox.Root>
```

### Controlled and changing items

The viewer's state is two values, each of which you can control: whether it is open, with `open` and `onOpenChange`, and the item it shows, with `value` and `onValueChange`. Here they are shown in an address bar, as an app would put them in the URL. The value stays on the last item shown after the viewer closes, and is the first item until one is set. Changing `value` while the viewer is open slides to the new item, as Next does. A trigger calls `onValueChange` when it shows another item, then `onOpenChange`; `handle.open(value)` does the same.

`items` can change at any time, even while the viewer is open: it stays on the same item. Delete the open photo from the viewer and it moves to the neighbour; delete the last one and it closes. Add photos and the counter follows.

<Demo src="./demos/manager.tsx" />

```tsx title="Controlled lightbox"
const [open, setOpen] = React.useState(false);
const [value, setValue] = React.useState<LightboxItemValue>(photos[0].id);

<Lightbox.Root
  items={photos}
  open={open}
  onOpenChange={setOpen}
  value={value}
  onValueChange={setValue}
>
  …
</Lightbox.Root>;
```

Both callbacks also receive `eventDetails`, built like Base UI's: `reason` tells what made the change (`"swipe"`, `"trigger-press"`, `"thumbnail-press"`, `"escape-key"`, `"close-press"` …), `event` is the native event and `trigger` the element it came from. Call `eventDetails.cancel()` to stop it: a cancelled swipe springs back, and a cancelled pull to dismiss puts the image back. `onOpenChangeComplete` is called once the image has landed, in the viewer (`true`) or back on the page (`false`).

```tsx title="Change reasons"
<Lightbox.Root
  items={photos}
  onOpenChange={(open, eventDetails) => {
    // A press outside the image keeps the viewer open; Escape and Close still close it.
    if (eventDetails.reason === "outside-press") eventDetails.cancel();
  }}
>
  …
</Lightbox.Root>
```

### Loading and errors

Before it opens, the viewer is mounted out of sight until the `<img>` or `<video>` in `Lightbox.Media` has loaded (a video, its size), so the image shows at once and flies at the right size. The trigger has `data-pending` meanwhile. The viewer opens anyway after `LIGHTBOX_OPEN_TIMEOUT` (3 s), still loading.

`onOpenChange` can return a Promise, and the viewer waits for it as well: here the slow photo waits 1.5 seconds more, as on a slow network. It waits up to 3 s before opening and 300 ms before closing (`LIGHTBOX_CLOSE_TIMEOUT`), then goes on. `eventDetails.cancel()` works until the wait ends, after an `await` too, and a cancelled change never happened: no `onOpenChangeComplete`, nothing to undo. A rejected Promise is logged, and the viewer goes on.

Inside the viewer, `Lightbox.Media` and its `Lightbox.Slide` have `data-loading` while the media loads and `data-error` if it fails, from the media's own events. Put a spinner or a message next to the media, inside `Lightbox.Media`: everything in it flies and follows the pull to dismiss. When the image fails, hide it (`group-data-error:invisible`): the box keeps its shape and the message takes its place. Give the `<img>` a new `src` to load it again. A failed image doesn't zoom, and the viewer keeps working.

- **Unknown size.** Without `width` and `height`, the shape is only known once the image loads: fade it in with `group-data-loading:opacity-0`.
- **Retry.** The fourth photo fails once and loads on retry. The message has `role="alert"`, so screen readers announce it.
- **Broken thumbnails.** When a photo is gone, its thumbnail fails too. Base UI's `Avatar` shows an icon in its place; its `delay` keeps a thumbnail that loads quickly from flashing the icon.

<Demo src="./demos/loading-and-errors.tsx" />

```tsx title="Loading and errors"
<Lightbox.Media width={photo.width} height={photo.height} className="group">
  <img src={photo.src} alt={photo.alt} className="group-data-error:invisible" />
  <span className="hidden group-data-loading:block">{/* spinner */}</span>
  <p className="hidden group-data-error:block">
    This image couldn’t be loaded.
  </p>
</Lightbox.Media>
```

`Lightbox.Media` only reads its direct `<img>`, `<video>` or `<iframe>` child (or the `<img>` of a `<picture>`), so an icon inside the message doesn't count. Content without one counts as loaded at once.

To show smaller images as thumbnails and larger ones in the viewer, give each `<img>` its own `src` or `srcSet`: the viewer waits for the one it shows.

### Video and other media

`Lightbox.Media` takes any media as its direct child: an `<img>`, a `<video>`, an `<iframe>`. It fits the media's shape inside the slide, flies it from the trigger and back, and follows the pull to dismiss. Only images zoom: on other media the zoom gestures do nothing and `Lightbox.ZoomIn` is disabled. A video pauses when its slide is no longer shown and when the viewer closes, and while a player with `controls` has focus, the arrow keys stay its own. Give the `<video>` `preload="metadata"`: the viewer opens once it knows the video's size.

<Demo src="./demos/video.tsx" />

Give an embed its `width` and `height` (`16` and `9`). An iframe keeps every touch to itself, so swipe beside it or use `Lightbox.Previous` and `Lightbox.Next`.

### Captions and animations

`Lightbox.Title` and `Lightbox.Description` name and describe the viewer for screen readers. A function child receives the item shown, to build its title or caption; `Lightbox.Description` renders nothing when the result is empty. For text that belongs to a slide and moves with it, put a `Lightbox.Caption` in the slide: it describes its slide.

When the item shown changes, `Lightbox.Title` and `Lightbox.Description` (with a function child), `Lightbox.Counter` and `Lightbox.Caption` have `data-starting-style` for one frame, with `data-activation-direction` set to the side the new item came from: `right` after Next in a left-to-right layout, `left` after Previous. Style where the new text starts from, and a transition brings it in, as in the gallery at the top of the page. The element stays on screen while its text changes, so turn the transition off while the attribute is there (`data-starting-style:transition-none`): the text jumps to its start, then animates in.

```tsx title="Animated captions"
<Lightbox.Description className="transition-[opacity,translate] duration-300 data-starting-style:opacity-0 data-starting-style:transition-none data-starting-style:data-[activation-direction=right]:translate-x-5 data-starting-style:data-[activation-direction=left]:-translate-x-5">
  {(photo) => photo.caption}
</Lightbox.Description>
```

`Lightbox.Counter` and `Lightbox.ZoomValue` take a function child, which receives their state, to format the position and the scale: ``{(state) => `${state.index + 1} of ${state.count}`}``.

### Thumbnails

`Lightbox.ThumbnailList` renders a `Lightbox.Thumbnail` per item inside the viewer, through a function child like `Lightbox.Viewport`. What's inside a thumbnail is yours: an image, a dot, a title. The list is a tab list and each thumbnail a tab, as in the WAI-ARIA tabbed carousel: the slides become their tab panels, and the thumbnail of the item shown has `aria-selected` and `data-active`.

- **Keyboard.** The list is a single tab stop. The arrow keys move between thumbnails and show their item, and Home and End go to the first and last. With `activateOnFocus={false}`, the arrows only move focus and Enter shows the item.
- **Orientation.** `orientation` sets which arrow keys move and which way the list scrolls: ← → for `"horizontal"` (the default), ↑ ↓ for `"vertical"`. The list has `data-orientation`, so one class list can style both.
- **Scrolling.** Give the list `overflow: auto`. The active thumbnail glides to its centre, and is centred again when a CSS transition changes its size. The mouse can drag the list (`data-swiping` meanwhile), and the wheel scrolls a horizontal list. Touch scrolls natively.
- **Edges.** The list has `data-overflow-start` and `data-overflow-end` while thumbnails are hidden past an edge: fade that edge with a mask, `data-overflow-end:mask-r-from-85%`. Hide the browser's scrollbar with `[scrollbar-width:none] [&::-webkit-scrollbar]:hidden`.
- **Sliding indicator.** `Lightbox.ThumbnailIndicator`, placed in the list after the function child, is one element that glides to the active thumbnail, like Base UI's `Tabs.Indicator`. Make the list `relative`: the indicator gets the active thumbnail's box in it as `--active-thumbnail-left`, `--active-thumbnail-top`, `--active-thumbnail-width` and `--active-thumbnail-height`, scrolls with the list, and follows thumbnails that change size. The variables move with the slides on every frame: towards the next or previous thumbnail while a swipe drags them, then along with them as they settle, at the speed of the flick, and the same for Previous, Next and the keys. So the indicator needs no transition: without one, it lands exactly when the slides do. A jump of several items (Home, End, a far thumbnail) starts the glide from the thumbnail next to the target.

```tsx title="Thumbnails"
<Lightbox.ThumbnailList className="relative flex gap-2 overflow-x-auto">
  {(photo) => (
    <Lightbox.Thumbnail aria-label={photo.alt}>
      <img src={photo.thumb} alt="" />
    </Lightbox.Thumbnail>
  )}
  <Lightbox.ThumbnailIndicator className="absolute top-(--active-thumbnail-top) left-(--active-thumbnail-left) h-(--active-thumbnail-height) w-(--active-thumbnail-width)" />
</Lightbox.ThumbnailList>
```

### Phone viewer

The viewer works like the photo app of a phone:

- **Swipe** between images; the image follows the finger, carries on with the flick, and rubber-bands at the first and last image.
- **Pinch** to zoom around the fingers, and **double tap** to zoom in at the tapped point or back out.
- **Pan** a zoomed image; past its edge, the drag carries on to the next image.
- **Pull down** (or up) to dismiss: the image follows the finger and flies back to its thumbnail on release.
- **Tap** to hide or show the controls.

With a mouse or trackpad, drag swipes, double click zooms, and the wheel or a trackpad pinch zooms around the cursor.

Here the image fills the screen width under translucent bars. While the image is pulled to dismiss, `--lightbox-dismiss-progress` goes from 0 to 1 on the popup and the backdrop: the page fades in and un-blurs behind it, and the bars slide off the top and bottom of the screen. Try it on a phone, or with the browser's device toolbar.

<Demo src="./demos/phone.tsx" />

```tsx title="Controls that slide away"
// The top bar slides up and out, faster than the drag goes on; no transition while swiping.
<header className="-translate-y-[calc((100%+1rem)*1.5*var(--lightbox-dismiss-progress,0))] transition-[translate] in-data-swiping:transition-none">
  …
</header>
```

### Styling

Every part is unstyled. Style the viewer with Tailwind (or any CSS), using the parts' data attributes and CSS variables:

- `Lightbox.Popup` has `data-zoomed` while the image is zoomed, `data-controls-hidden` after a tap hid the controls, and `data-starting-style` / `data-ending-style` while the image flies in and out. Fade the controls, not the popup: the image is inside it. From a control, read them with `in-data-*`: `in-data-controls-hidden:opacity-0`. Its `className`, `style` and `render` functions receive `zoomed`, `controlsHidden` and `activationDirection` with the dialog's state.
- `Lightbox.Popup`, `Lightbox.Viewport`, `Lightbox.Slide` and the parts that show the item have `data-activation-direction` (`left`, `right`, or `none` when the viewer opens): the side the item shown came from, mirrored in a right-to-left layout.
- `Lightbox.Popup` gets `--lightbox-swipe-movement-x` (px on screen, negative to the left) and `--lightbox-swipe-progress` (the same as a share of a slide, `-1` to `1`) while a swipe drags the slides and while they settle, like Base UI's drawer swipe variables. They are `0` at rest. Move anything with the swipe: `translate-x-[calc(var(--lightbox-swipe-progress,0)*2rem)]`. They are relative to the item shown, so they jump when it changes at the end of a swipe.
- `Lightbox.Popup` gets `--lightbox-swipe-strength` when a swipe is released: `0.1` for a hard flick to `1` for a slow release, like Base UI's drawer. The slides settle in 300 ms times it, and go back to `1` once settled: scale a transition that follows them, `duration-[calc(300ms*var(--lightbox-swipe-strength,1))]`.
- `Lightbox.Popup` gets `--lightbox-zoom-scale` (`1` at rest) and `--lightbox-zoom-progress` (`0` at rest, `1` at the largest scale), to fade or move things as the image zooms.
- `Lightbox.Popup` and `Lightbox.Backdrop` get `--lightbox-flight-duration` and `--lightbox-flight-easing`: the length and curve of the flight the image is on. After a pull to dismiss, the curve is a spring (a CSS `linear()`) that starts at the speed of the finger. Time the backdrop and the controls with them, `duration-(--lightbox-flight-duration) ease-(--lightbox-flight-easing)`, and they arrive with the image.
- `Lightbox.Popup` gets `--lightbox-flight-movement-x` and `--lightbox-flight-movement-y` (px) and `--lightbox-flight-scale` at the start of each flight: how far from where it rests in the viewer the image flies, to the trigger it lands on when the viewer closes, or from the trigger it leaves when it opens, and its size there as a share of its size in the viewer. They are `0px` and `1` before any flight. Send other content to the same place: `[translate:var(--lightbox-flight-movement-x)_var(--lightbox-flight-movement-y)] scale-(--lightbox-flight-scale)` on `data-ending-style`, timed with `--lightbox-flight-duration`, as the coverflow does.
- `Lightbox.Popup` and `Lightbox.Backdrop` get `--lightbox-dismiss-movement-x` and `--lightbox-dismiss-movement-y` (px) with `--lightbox-dismiss-progress` (0 to 1) while the image is pulled to dismiss, and have `data-swiping` during a drag and until the image settles back. Turn transitions off then (`data-swiping:transition-none`, or `in-data-swiping:transition-none` on a control), or whatever follows the progress lags behind the finger.
- `Lightbox.Viewport` has `data-swiping` during a gesture. Give it a size (`absolute inset-0`) and set the space between images with `gap-x-4`. Nothing clips: the next images, the zoomed image and the image pulled to dismiss are drawn past its edges, off screen in a full-screen viewer. In a smaller one, add `overflow-hidden`; leave it off for the media to leave the viewport, like the stories. One slide of finger travel is its width plus that gap; when the CSS draws the slides closer together, set `--lightbox-swipe-distance` on it (any CSS length) so they keep up with the finger, as the coverflow does.
- `Lightbox.Slide` has `--lightbox-slide-progress`: its distance from the centre in slides, on every frame the slides move. It is placed with `translate`, leaving `transform` free for a custom transition (a cube, a fade, a stack); put a `perspective` on `Lightbox.Viewport` for 3D.
- `Lightbox.Slide`'s padding is the space around the media: `p-0` for edge to edge on phones, `md:px-24` on larger screens. Add `pt-[env(safe-area-inset-top)]` and the like for notches (with `viewport-fit=cover` on the page). It has `data-active`, and `data-loading` and `data-error` for its media.
- `Lightbox.Media` sets its own position and size, and the size of the media in it: style what's around the media, not its box.
- `Lightbox.Trigger` has `data-popup-open` while the image will fly back to it, from the opening flight to the end of the closing one: the trigger of the item shown, or the one `closeTarget` picks. Hide it with `data-popup-open:invisible`: the thumbnail disappears from the page but keeps its place, and comes back when the image lands. It also has `data-pending` while the viewer waits to open from it, and `data-flying` while the image flies from it or to it.

### Keyboard

| Key        | Action                                       |
| :--------- | :------------------------------------------- |
| ← →        | Previous / next image, or pan while zoomed   |
| ↑ ↓        | Pan while zoomed                             |
| Home / End | First / last image                           |
| + −        | Zoom in / out                                |
| 0          | Reset the zoom                               |
| Esc        | Close; the image flies back to its thumbnail |

Inside a widget that uses the arrow keys itself, they stay that widget's: a Base UI `Toolbar`, `Lightbox.ThumbnailList`, a slider, a text field, a video player.
